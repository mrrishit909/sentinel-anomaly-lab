# Limitations

1. **The telemetry is simulated.** CATS is a simulator output with deliberately injected anomalies, "provided without noise" (dataset document). Nothing here is evidence about performance on real server, spacecraft or industrial telemetry, where noise, drift, missing data, mislabelled boundaries and changing normal behaviour dominate. All telemetry and labels are tagged SYNTHETIC.
2. **Dataset change.** The plan named SMD. It was dropped because it has no stated dataset licence (`DATA_SOURCES.md`). Any copy that says "real telemetry" or "SMD" is wrong.
3. **One system, one run.** A single 57.9-day series, 200 incidents, one random seed per stochastic method. With 67 test incidents, an event recall of 0.612 has a 95% interval of roughly plus or minus 0.12. Differences of a few incidents between detectors are not significant. No cross-validation across time blocks was done.
4. **Thresholds do not transfer perfectly.** Thresholds are tuned on val. False alerts per day on test differ from val (for example robust z-score at the 1-per-day budget: 0.97 on val, 2.0 on test). The browser threshold lab shows both.
5. **Label timing is exact; real labels are not.** The ground truth marks start and end to the second. Delay numbers are therefore cleaner than they would be on human-labelled data.
6. **Point-adjust is not a quality statement.** It is reported only to show how much it inflates (up to 18.6x F1 here). Do not compare the point-adjusted numbers with papers that use different protocols.
7. **Event metric parameters are choices.** `merge_gap_s = 30` and `tol_after_s = 60` shape false alerts per day and event precision. Changing them changes the numbers; they are published in `meta.metric_params`.
8. **Change-point is made causal by a delay.** Its latency (about 100 s of look-ahead plus bin completion) is part of the method here; an offline segmentation would be more accurate and not usable for alerting.
9. **GBM scope.** It is trained on 83 incidents and sees PCA outputs; it is supervised and cannot be fitted without labelled incidents. Its score is a probability under a 5% prevalence training mix and is not calibrated for deployment.
10. **Isolation Forest is weak here** (average precision 0.19 on test) because anomalies are largely contextual relations between channels rather than rare points in channel space. This is a result, not a tuning failure we hid: only its sample size and feature set were fixed before looking at results.
11. **Attribution covers 10 telemetry channels** and scores against one `root_cause` and one `affected` channel per incident. The context channels (commands and stimuli) are never candidates. Rankings show which measurements moved, not why.
12. **Published slices.** Only 12 incident windows (at most 3,600 rows, 16-bit quantised) are shipped. Per-detector scores for the whole series are shipped only as 600 s max-pooled overview bins; the threshold lab's global numbers come from the precomputed sweep (160 thresholds), and the live recompute from stored scores operates on the 12 windows. Raw data is not redistributed.
13. **Command versus stimulus roles** of the seven context channels are not assigned (the dataset's figure was not machine readable here).
14. **Footer wording** in the dataset PDF ("Commercial in Confidence") conflicts with its explicit CC BY 4.0 grant; we rely on the grant and the Zenodo licence field (`DATA_SOURCES.md`).
